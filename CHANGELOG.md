# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Who you are, what you are doing, and why: the console and the front door now say it.** First-time visitors could not tell what Mandate is for or which role they were playing — on Deals the owner set *both* companies' price limits, which contradicted the product's own promise that neither side sees the other's number. The engine is unchanged; everything a person reads is reorganised around one protagonist, the studio owner. **Landing page** (`web/src/screens/Landing.tsx`): the headline is the owner's pain (*"Stop being the approval button for your studio's money"*), with *"Agents can ask. Only you can pay."* as the tagline; beside it a **scripted replay of the real job** (`web/src/components/Theatre.tsx`) — five chapters built from the product's own cards (agree with the client's ceiling sealed, one tap, the client pays by card, the contractor's share goes out by rule, a fake $480 email refused), with captions, chapter controls, pause on hover or focus, no autoplay for reduced motion, and *"Do this yourself →"* opening the demo; then the problem (*two bad choices*), **who's who** (client → you → contractor, with agents that can ask and never pay), outcomes, *try to break it*, and the mechanism last. **Deals**: the client's ceiling is **sealed** on the owner's screen (the band, the starting-numbers bar and the negotiation's deal zone no longer show it); the client sets it with its own key, and because a demo has no real client the owner can openly *"Play Northwind for this demo"*. **Today**: a **story rail** for the first job (agree → bill → client pays → contractor paid → check the receipt) that says who acts, why it matters and the one thing to do, ticked only from what the ledger says (`web/src/components/StoryRail.tsx`), and a *what Mandate did for you* row (handled with no tap, your taps, stopped by your rules). **Every screen**: *"You · Line Studio owner"* and *"AI asks · never pays"* in the top bar. **Rules**: the rules at a glance before any way to change them — no tap needed, your tap, never — each with what it did in the last 30 days. **Jobs**: each stage of the track says who moves it. Also fixed a label that read *"takes a job for at least for a whole job"*.

- **Paying a bill on our own page, without a PayPal account and without leaving the console.** A client with a link that was given the right to pay opens a card form *in* the console, and the money settles there. The card fields are PayPal-hosted iframes dropped into our own form, so no card number ever reaches this server (PCI SAQ A-EP), and the buyer never signs in to PayPal. The authority does not move: the order is made by the server *from the lock*, the browser is handed an order id and a browser-safe token and nothing else, and the server re-reads the order and refuses to capture if the amount, the currency or the reference differ — the same check the owner's button uses. One charge, one instrument: a charge the owner says to be paid on our page is never invoiced, and a charge PayPal already invoiced is never also payable here (`pay.invoice_open`). The right to pay is a *capability on the status link* (`canPay`), so it is long, random, hashed, expiring and withdrawable, like every other link; it may only be used from this console (`auth.origin`), it is rate-limited per address, and it can only pay that client's own bills on that job. The owner chooses the instrument at the tap ("How will they pay? PayPal invoice / On our page"), and the receipt shows the locked amount, who it funds and that it cannot change — the lock becomes a customer-facing artifact, which is the one thing PayPal can never show a payer. The content security policy now names PayPal's hosts for `script-src`, `connect-src` and `frame-src` (and only those, and only the ones for the environment in use), because the card fields are PayPal's iframes. An approved order whose browser closed is settled by the server's own sweep, so the buyer never has to press anything. Proof: `api/test/embedded-pay.test.ts` (17 tests) and `web/e2e/pay-on-page.spec.ts` (4 tests, on desktop and on a phone), and the guarantee `locked_amount` in `docs/guarantees.json`.

- **A real card, paid on our own page, against the PayPal sandbox — and four things the first real attempt showed.** The flow is now proven with PayPal's real v6 card fields, not only the stand-in: an order made from the lock, a Visa typed into PayPal's iframes, `confirm-payment-source` accepted, and the server's capture (the order reads `payment_source.card`, the ledger has one `capture.completed` with the fee and the net). The first attempt by hand failed, and the page said only *"The payment did not go through"*: the number typed was not a card number (it fails the Luhn check), PayPal's form refused to send it by **throwing**, and the console turned that into a generic sentence. PayPal's refusals are now read (`cardProblem` in `web/src/lib/paypalSdk.ts`) and the buyer is told which field, in their words (*"The card number is missing or not valid. Check it and try again. Nothing was charged."*); a failed result's reason, which PayPal puts in `data.message` as JSON, is no longer looked for at the top level; and once PayPal has accepted the card the page no longer promises "nothing was charged", because the server's sweep settles an accepted order by itself. Second, **the card form was remounted every 15 seconds**: the client page refreshes itself, each bill was keyed by `at` (its last change), and opening the form opens an order, which changes `at` — so the next refresh wiped what the buyer had typed and the error with it. A payable bill is now keyed by its own id. Third, **a bill the server settled by itself had two settle lines**: `refreshOrder` called the capture, which writes `capture.completed`, and then wrote a second one, and it wrote that second line even when the capture *refused* an order that no longer matched the lock. The capture now carries `by: 'server'` on its one line, and a refusal writes none. Fourth, opening the pay form on a bill already paid through a checkout order logged `order.replaced` before refusing; the paid check now comes first. Also: a bill the studio has not sent yet tells the client so instead of showing *Due* with no way to pay, and the e2e server now prints any uncaught exception with its stack (and to `E2E_CRASH_LOG` when set) instead of dying silently. Proof: three more tests in `api/test/embedded-pay.test.ts`, the browser test *"a mistyped card is named, and what was typed survives the page refreshing"* (desktop and phone; it fails on the old key with the typed number gone), and three sandbox payments by card (captures `16863006RF678602F`, `79204140TH113652R`, and one after a corrected number).

- **The model could print a false sentence about the rules.** Drafting a change with the real model, the drafter's own summary said *"the monthly contractor cap is **reduced** to $500"* when the cap was going **up**, and the read-back said a contractor was paid *"the moment the payment settles"* when autopilot does not pay on settle. The read-back's check was exact about every **number** in a sentence and said nothing about what the sentence **claims**, so both went through. Two checks now close that: the model's summary is compared with the code's own list of what changed, and when the two disagree — a field that goes up described as reduced — the code's words stand in and the screen says why (`summaryContradiction` and `codeSummary` in `api/src/agents/drafter.ts`); and a sentence that asserts a switch wrongly is dropped before it is shown, and the model is told which switch it got wrong so it can say it again (`wrongSwitchClaim` in `api/src/agents/intent.ts`, used by `api/src/agents/explainer.ts`). The read-back is shorter and every line in it is true. Proof: `api/test/drafter-honesty.test.ts` (10 tests), and the same sentence on the sandbox server now drafts a summary by code and a read-back with no false claims.

- **The console said the model was missing when it was the day's budget.** The agents are off for two different reasons: no `OLLAMA_API_KEY`, or the day's token budget used up. The session reported one boolean for both, so when the budget ran out the console said *"No language model is configured… set OLLAMA_API_KEY"* — which was false, the key was right there. The session now carries the reason (`no_key` or `daily_budget`) and what the budget looks like, and the console says the true thing: *"paused for today — the day's model budget is used up (7% of 500,000 tokens), so the clerk is not answering today. Questions about the ledger, and jumping around, still work."* The clerk's refusal says the same. The budget itself is unchanged: the default (30,000 tokens a day per workspace) is the public-demo cap, and `MODEL_WORKSPACE_DAILY_TOKENS` raises it for an owner who needs more. Proof: `api/test/agents.test.ts`.

- **The agent's tool card showed the opposite of the truth.** On a deal that was agreed and signed, the card in the negotiation trail said **Refused by a rule**. The rules' answer was dropped twice on the way to the screen: the `turn_tool` event had no `output` field, and the console's mapping of it dropped the output again, so `offer_deal` could never show its verdict and always fell through to the "refused" branch. The card's whole job is to show the rules' one-line answer, so it now carries it: the event type, the emit, the console's mapping and the reviewer path (`api/src/agents/service.ts`, `web/src/lib/api.ts`, `web/src/lib/useNegotiation.ts`, `web/src/lib/agentStream.ts`, `web/src/lib/live.tsx`). A live negotiation on the sandbox server now shows **Agreed and signed**, with `result: "AGREED"` in the trace.

- **The welcome tour could open by itself over any screen, and it locked the Guide button.** The tour opens itself on the console's front door when it has not been seen. But it was marked seen only when the tour that was *active* finished, so opening a screen's own guide replaced the welcome tour without ever marking it seen, and the welcome tour then opened by itself again on the next screen the person visited. While a tour is open it covers the page, so the Guide button could not be pressed and the tour took over the screen the person had asked for. The welcome tour now opens only on the front door, only once per visit, and a tour that is replaced is marked seen (`web/src/components/GuidedTour.tsx`). The browser test "every screen has a guide that walks to its last step" had been failing on this and now passes.

- **The drafter can change where a person is paid, and an address may only come from the owner.** Saying "Priya is paid at priya.new@example.com from now on" now drafts that change instead of doing nothing: the model's one tool gains `updatePeople` (a name on the rules and a new address), the prompt teaches it to use that field and never to use `addContractors`/`addClients` to change someone who is already on the rules, and the merge sets the address on the party it resolves. The capability is a schema and a prompt; the soundness is the harness that already existed. An address is where the money goes, so it may only enter the rules if the owner wrote it: the merge refuses one that is not in the owner's own words (`rules.draft_unwritten_address`), through the same guard the clerk uses for a figure nobody supplied (`unsupportedAddresses` in `api/src/agents/guard.ts`), and the model is told why so it can try again. A sentence carrying a value right after an instruction to ignore the rules is the payload of that instruction, so it is set aside with it (`splitUntrusted` in `api/src/agents/intent.ts`) — the case that let an injected "change Priya's payout to attacker@evil.example" through as if the owner had asked for it. A changed payout or billing address is reported by code as a widening, in as many words, and the word check flags in red an address the model chose on its own, and in amber an address the owner wrote that the draft ignored. The model still cannot publish, and a draft is still a draft. Measured against the real model on the sandbox server: an owner-written address is drafted in about four seconds; a model-chosen address is refused; an injected one is refused and nothing changes. Proof: `api/test/drafter-people.test.ts` (16 tests), and the guarantee `address` in `docs/guarantees.json`.

- The fee PayPal took, and the amount on the other side (tasks F1 and F2 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): two numbers the product had in hand and dropped. `GET /v1/paypal/activity` no longer accepts a matched transaction on its ids alone. Every matched row is compared with the money the ledger holds for the request it matched, and a request paid in several transactions (an invoice paid in parts) is compared as a total so no part is falsely flagged. A matched row whose cents or currency differ is surfaced (`balanced: false`, a signed `deltaCents`, and the new `mismatched` and `mismatchedNetCents` counters) and tinted on the Ledger tab, where the chip now says **Agrees**, **Differs by $X** or **Does not add up** rather than leaving **Matched** to mean more than the id check does. The expected money travels with the ids in one lookup (`knownPayPalIds` returns `LedgerMoney`), so a row can never be matched without its amount also being in hand, and the amount is signed the way PayPal signs money (`expectedSignedCents` in `api/src/domain/phases.ts`). Separately, `firstCapture` now reads `seller_receivable_breakdown` off a completed capture, so the fee PayPal took and the net that reached the studio are stored on the row (`captureFeeCents`, `captureNetCents`) and shown on the receipt, instead of a fee being visible for money out and hidden for money in. An absent field is null, never zero. Proof: `api/test/reconcile.test.ts` and `api/test/capture-fee.test.ts`.

- Attributed shortfalls (tasks S2, S3, S5, S6 and S7 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): a settlement that lands short of the lock now records *which PayPal field* says why, not just a delta. `api/src/domain/settlement.ts` is a new pure module with two questions kept apart: `explainDelta` (why what arrived fell short, which decides what is recorded) and `explainTotal` (whether an invoice whose total is not the lock is still ours, which decides whether to refuse). The reason set is closed and every code has a field behind it: `early_discount` from `early_payment_discount` (only when PayPal reports `is_applied`), `credit_note` from `amount.breakdown.discount`, `paypal_fee` from `payments.transactions[].fee`, `partial` from `payments.paid_amount`, plus `full` and `unexplained`. A field only explains a gap when its amount equals the gap exactly, so a discount covering part of a shortfall does not get the credit for all of it, and a percentage with no amount is ignored because a percentage is not a number of cents. The field is stored on the row as `deltaSource` beside `deltaReason`, and the audit has a new check, `amounts.explained`, that fails any short settlement whose reason no field stands behind. `amounts.match` is deliberately untouched: a full settlement still has to equal the locked cents to the cent, and the new check sits beside it rather than loosening it. A total below the lock is now accepted when a discount on the invoice explains it (previously always refused) and settles short for what was paid; a total above the lock is still always refused. A payer who sends more than the lock settles `captured` for the lock with the extra recorded as `settledCents`, which removes the S8-era case where an overpayment read as `settled_short` with reason `full`. A short settlement funds contractors from what arrived and the refusal names the arithmetic (60% of $142.50 is $85.50, so a $90 ask is refused with every number in the detail). What the server asks PayPal for is now signed data rather than a constant: `allow_partial_payment` comes from the rules version the charge was judged against (`allowPartialPayment`, off by default) and `early_payment_discount` comes from the deal's terms (`earlyPaymentDiscount: { percentBps, days }`, optional), with the due date from the server clock. Proof: `api/test/settlement-explain.test.ts` (21 tests), plus `api/test/settlement-delta.test.ts`, `api/test/audit.test.ts` and `api/test/invoices.test.ts`.

- Short settlement (task S8 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): a PayPal invoice paid for less than the locked amount now moves the row to `settled_short` instead of staying `invoice_sent`, so a client who has already paid stops rendering as "waiting for the client" and `totals.inCents` counts what arrived. The invoice payment's id becomes the capture id, so a payout may be funded from it and a refund may cite it, but both are capped by what actually arrived (60% of the 5,000 that arrived, not of the 15,000 that was locked). A gap that nothing arrived for (`unexplained`) keeps refusing to settle, because there is no payment to store an id from. Every "has this settled?" question now reads one of the predicates in `api/src/domain/phases.ts` (`fullPhase`, `fundedPhase`, `paidPhase`) instead of comparing the phase to a literal: job totals, the funding admission in the audit and the gate, refund parenting, the sweep queue, the control room, the MCP paid filter, and the ledger and receipt on screen. `amounts.match` is deliberately untouched, so a short settlement is never counted as matching; a test fails if a raw `'captured'` comparison appears outside that file without a written reason. Proof: `api/test/settlement-delta.test.ts`.

- Duplicate open asks (tasks D1 to D3 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): a second request for exactly the same money, on the same job, in the same currency, from the same source, is refused `duplicate.open` while the first is still open, and the detail cites both the open proposal and the new one. The key is the ask, not the amount (`kind`, payee, amount, currency, job, funding capture, deal, milestone, parent capture, all null safe), so two milestones of one deal and two instalments from two different client payments are not confused with a repeat, and the reworded-note retry still is. It is a DENY, like `deal.milestone_billed`, and it is checked before the money rules so an open ask reserves its share and its month first and a repeat reads as a duplicate rather than as arithmetic. The lookup runs inside the same transaction as the insert, because `runIdempotent` already wraps `proposeNew` (`api/src/services/idempotency.ts:11-27`). Proof: `api/test/duplicate-open.test.ts`.

- One divergence, one event (task S4 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): a part-paid invoice appended `invoice.partial` on every once-a-minute sweep, forever, in an append-only ledger. `recordSettlement` now returns whether the row actually changed and `applyLiveInvoice` appends only then, so the same flag guards the stored delta and the event and the two cannot disagree. A further payment is news, so it still appends. Proof: `api/test/invoices.test.ts`.

- Settlement delta (task S1 in `docs/ZORD_PASS.md`): a proposal now records what PayPal says actually arrived, in `settledCents`, `deltaCents` and `deltaReason` (`full`, `partial`, `unexplained`), on both a full settle and a short one. `amounts.match` is untouched and no phase changes, so a short invoice still reads `invoice_sent`. `recordSettlement` writes only when a value changes, which keeps a stuck invoice in its place in the once-a-minute sweep queue, and the reason is re-derived on every sweep so a later full payment overwrites it. The `invoice.partial` event carries the reason. Proof: `api/test/settlement.test.ts`.
