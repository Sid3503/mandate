Mandate

AI agents that negotiate, agree and settle money for you, inside rules you signed.

PayPal AI Hackathon 2026 PayPal Orders + Payouts · open-weights AI · MCP

Open with the simplest promise: Mandate separates asking from paying. In today’s demo, the agent can help a small studio move faster, but the money only moves when a deterministic warrant allows it and, when required, Meera taps. This is not a wallet, not a new rail, and not a replacement for PayPal. It is a spend-authority layer in front of PayPal that turns every AI-assisted payment into a constrained, explainable packet.

The shift · agentic commerce

Agents can already negotiate. They can’t safely settle.

The next layer of commerce is agent to agent. Money is the step nobody has made safe.

NextAgents negotiate → bill → collect → pay the contractor

Here’s the shift. Today, AI recommends and a human pays. The next layer of commerce is agent to agent: your agent finds a designer’s agent, they negotiate scope, price and milestones, then they bill, collect and pay. PayPal’s own hackathon brief asks for exactly this. The language part already works. The money part is what nobody has made safe: who gave this agent permission to spend, and how much? Today the only answer is to give it the keys, or to make a human do every step.

Agentic payment demos are exciting because the model can read context and take action. The dangerous version is when the same model reads untrusted vendor messages and also carries payment tools with credentials. A malicious or compromised email does not need to break PayPal. It only needs to steer the model that holds the client secret. Mandate changes the shape: the model can propose, but it cannot authorize, lower policy, or call PayPal directly.

This is not a hypothetical risk created by AI. Business Email Compromise is already one of the most expensive internet crime categories, with $2.77 billion in reported losses and 21,442 complaints in the FBI IC3 2024 Internet Crime Report. AI changes the economics. It makes vendor impersonation cheaper, more fluent, and easier to personalize. So the control point cannot be the model’s judgment alone. The control point has to be deterministic payment authority.

The insight

The user who creates a payout cannot approve the same payout.

PayPal does this for people

Inside a dashboard, creation and approval are split.

Mandate does this for agents

At the API, before PayPal is called.

The key idea came from PayPal’s own Payouts approval model: the person who creates a payout cannot approve the same payout. That is a beautiful separation-of-duties principle. Mandate takes that principle from a human dashboard and makes it programmable for agents. The AI can assemble a request, cite evidence, and explain why it thinks the payment fits. But the warrant gate decides whether the request is denied, auto-settled, or sent to Meera for approval.

This is the idea no one else has. Each company writes its own rules once. Northwind: design work, at most four hundred dollars. The studio: a logo costs at least two-fifty. The agents can say anything, but the server only accepts terms inside the overlap. The studio agent’s four-fifty is refused, Northwind’s two hundred is refused, three hundred is accepted. The model never decides whether a deal is allowed; a pure function does. That makes agent-to-agent commerce safe by design, not by prompt.

The whole system in one line. Agents, ours or anyone’s, connect only to Mandate’s MCP server, which has three tools: propose, list and explain. Nothing that pays. Every proposal hits a rules gate written in plain code, checked against both sides’ rules, with three possible answers. If the owner needs to approve, her tap makes the server sign a lock that fixes the payee, the cents and the proof. Only then does Mandate call PayPal, through PayPal’s Agent Toolkit, Orders or Payouts. A verified webhook updates the ledger, and the receipt explains every dollar. No agent ever holds the token.

Four AI roles. Northwind’s buyer agent and the studio’s sales agent negotiate. The clerk is Arun’s chat for payouts. The reader handles untrusted emails and evidence with no tools at all.
